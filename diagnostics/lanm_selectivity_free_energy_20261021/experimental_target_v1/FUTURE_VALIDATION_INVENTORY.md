# Future validation candidates: metadata-only follow-up

21 October 2026. No affinity-table outcomes from the candidates below were displayed in this follow-up. This is an eligibility inventory, not a validation result.

## Preferred lead: LBTv / 1TJB

Primary publication: Nitz et al. (2004), [Structural Origin of the High Affinity of a Chemically Evolved Lanthanide-Binding Peptide](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.200460028).

Primary [PDB 1TJB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1TJB) metadata verifies:

- Sequence `YIDTNNDGWYEGDELLA(NH2)`, including explicit terminal amide.
- Seventeen amino acids, different from LBT3 at positions 1 and 10.
- Author-defined assembly 1 is monomeric. Two peptide chains in the asymmetric unit are not by themselves evidence for a biological dimer.
- Primary DOI and authors match the cited experimental study.

The existing 2020 LBT3 article identifies this older LBTv work as the source of a lanthanide-series affinity comparison. The exact Dy value, assay conditions, confidence intervals and free-ion convention were not opened here. Eligibility for a numerical La/Dy comparison remains contingent on those details.

Prior exposure is material: the general heavy-ion preference and La/Tb values already appeared in the consumed 2020 article and old project notes. Therefore, even if its Dy number remains concealed until protocol freeze, this is **external quantitative validation with partial outcome exposure**, not a fully blind sign test. Its close relationship to LBT3 also limits claims of transfer to unrelated folds.

Recommended next step: freeze the development protocol and prediction before a separate evidence reviewer extracts the full assay and affinity pair. Use fixed inclusion criteria based on chemical identity, matched conditions, equilibrium interpretation and uncertainty; do not choose between variants based on prediction agreement. If the exact La/Dy pair or sufficient assay metadata is absent, report ineligibility and select another independent case rather than inventing a label.
