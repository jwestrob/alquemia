MODEL CHANGE DECISION — 23 October 2026

Decision

Close the radial14 tuning strategy. Develop a short-range, geometry-resolved
metal–ligand interaction energy with separate two-body and three-body terms
and electrical response generated by that same energy. Retain the long-range
polarizable baseline. Address water and protein donors together.

This is a change in the interaction law, not a switch to another MACE checkpoint,
another radius/damping fit, or an energy offset attached to the old score.
The evidence is sufficient to choose this development direction. It is not
sufficient to declare the new model accurate, identify a unique microscopic
cause, or calculate protein-versus-water selectivity yet.

Why this decision follows from the existing results

1. The matched common-basis W8/W9 contrast has a pair-sum error +1.576 and a
   collective error -6.239 kcal/mol. The net error hides opposing contributions.
   Matching fragment bases did not remove the collective discrepancy.
2. The radial14 terminal improves its fitting objective, but all eight individual
   three-body energy AND dipole errors worsen relative to its starting candidate.
   All four Dy-minus-La three-body energy contrasts have the wrong sign. Passing
   its old 25 gates did not establish accurate electronic response: several
   electrical gates were non-worsening ceilings, not accuracy requirements.
3. The selected donor model gives a La acetate rotation slope -20.39, versus
   +28.36 with PBE0-D4 and +29.55 with canonical CCSD(T), in kcal/mol per A of the
   prescribed generalized coordinate. This large sign error survives a reference
   method change. Rotation also changes metal–O distances; it is not proof of a
   uniquely angular cause.
4. Electrical observations are independently wrong. For the measured La donor
   mode, the moment is too small (.114 versus .580 ea0) while susceptibility is
   already too large (54.13 versus about47.88 a0^3). An energy-only correction
   cannot repair these derivatives; uniformly increasing polarizability is also
   insufficient. Water shows related pair/collective cancellation.

These are actual model failures, not just optimizer convergence complaints.
The latest small feasible step improved the objective another0.407%; that does
not answer them. Conversely, failed local searches do not prove that every
possible parameter vector in the old family must fail.

What is restricted in the current model

The baseline already has many-body polarization and angular dipole interactions.
Its added fitted correction has radial driving fields and an isotropic response
change on the metal. At equal metal–O radii, its added scalar coefficients cannot
independently distinguish near and far ligand arrangements. Native vector/tensor
interactions still distinguish them; calling the entire model angle-free would
be false. The fitted nonpair correction is routed through quadratic induction.
That forces other missing short-range electronic contributions into the same
response mechanism, even though quantum nonadditivity is not uniquely induction.

Simple oxygen stiffness is not an untried solution. Positive radial EXCHPOL
probes failed; the donor radial-drive/isotropic-oxygen-stiffness probes also
traded improvements against worse mechanical predictions. Those results remain
part of the evidence. Additional ligand-frame/neighbor-angle tensors would be
a real extension, but by themselves still force the energy repair through
induction. I choose the broader energy-and-response architecture below.

Concrete successor Hamiltonian

Use intact chemical fragments: a metal M and a ligand L (water, acetate or amide).
Two oxygens of one chelating acetate belong to ONE ligand; they are not two
independent ligands in a three-body expansion.

  U_new = U_base + sum_(M,L) Delta U2 + sum_(M,L,L') Delta U3.

Delta terms are residuals against the SAME frozen baseline, with baseline
two-/three-body contributions subtracted explicitly. This avoids counting native
polarization twice. The current radial14 correction is not silently stacked
under a new residual. Exact baseline files must be bound once before numerical
development; the archived common-basis model is the starting baseline authority.

Terms depend on distances AND ligand orientation/ligand-pair arrangement, with
equivalent atoms/fragments treated symmetrically and smooth separation limits.
They cannot be functions only of metal–O radius or a scalar coordination count.
The three-body term vanishes when either ligand separates and is absent in a
dimer. The two-body sector is necessary because pair and donor errors also exist.

A concrete implementation retains the native two-channel stationary functional:

  L = U_nonpolar,base + sum_b Delta V_b(R_b)
      + K/2 [u^T G v - u^T(Ep+h) - v^T(Ed+h)],
  G = A0 + sum_b Delta H_b(R_b),
  h = sum_b Delta h_b(R_b),                 b = metal-containing pairs/triples.

Delta V supplies an independent short-range mechanical energy. Delta h is a
distributed, ligand-frame driving field; Delta H is a symmetric response
correction with ligand-frame and neighbor-angle dependence. The induced
variables u and v retain the original unequal native field conventions.
The pair/triple classification describes the local corrections BEFORE electronic
relaxation. Their coupled solve can generate higher-body effects. Actual quantum
two-/three-body targets must therefore be compared with the complete matching
fragment cycles of the new model, not equated to an isolated coefficient or V
term. Baseline subtraction defines residual targets; it does not license adding
the same fitted correction a second time after relaxation.
This is an effective energy model: V is not claimed to be pure Pauli repulsion,
h pure charge transfer, or H a uniquely measured quantum polarizability.

Unlike the current family, this representation can change short-range energy
and mechanical slopes without having to fake them entirely through induction,
while independently representing directional electrical response. All forces,
frame torques, dipoles and susceptibilities must be derivatives of this ONE
stationary energy. For reference interpretation the local field expansion is
Delta U = Delta V - Delta mu dot F - (1/2) F^T Delta alpha F + ... . These are
conjugate derivatives, not independently attached dipole predictions.

Actual environment fields must participate in the coupled response solve.
An isolated uniform-field Taylor fit appended only for reporting would NOT be
a transferable bulk Hamiltonian. Full coupled stability and separation limits
are required; locally positive response factors alone do not establish them.
There is no evidence here requiring nonlinear saturation or explicit charge
flow as additional electronic variables. Do not add those by assertion.

What to do next, in order

1. Reuse the existing matched pair, common-basis, trimer, donor radial/rotation,
   wet-minus-dry and qualified field datasets as one development collection.
   Keep baseline versions, methods, D4 conventions and failed references explicit.
   Use raw individual quantities as well as paired contrasts; derived contrasts
   are not independent observations. Do not fit only the final cancellation.
2. Implement one finite two-/three-body basis for Delta V, Delta h and Delta H
   with the stated symmetry, directions and separation behavior. Write its
   derivative and local-field coupling paths before selecting coefficients.
   Water and donors must share this framework; do not spend another cycle
   perfecting a water-only scalar score while donor forces remain wrong.
3. Make one declared, bounded development attempt using the accumulated data.
   Judge individual energies, forces/torques, signed moments and admitted
   susceptibility modes together. Retain existing physical accuracy targets and
   report reference-method disagreement separately. An improved aggregate loss
   cannot excuse a wrong force direction or compensating individual errors.
   If it fails, report the incompatible observables and reconsider the form;
   no automatic sequence of added radial parameters or relaxed safeguards.
4. Only a successful finite demonstration earns transfer/dynamics work. Existing
   data are consumed development data, not a new blind validation set. Coverage
   is incomplete: eight trimers are not a global three-body surface, and the
   uniform-field projections do not identify full response tensors. Any later
   data request must address a specified missing direction/domain. No generic
   new quantum comparison campaign is the immediate next step.
5. A transferable potential still needs solvated protein and aqueous-ion
   sampling to obtain the goal quantity: protein La-to-Dy free energy minus
   aqueous La-to-Dy free energy, with the required chemical-state/restraint terms.
   No current cluster score or periodic numerical certificate supplies that.

Scope, uncertainty and execution

This turn performed saved-evidence/source review and saved this decision; no
new fit, native molecular call, quantum calculation or numerical replay was
launched. Existing reference precision work may finish and remains preserved.
Do not automatically resume radial fitting or periodic-gradient development.
The existing periodic implementation remains useful archived engineering work,
but does not establish physical adequacy of its candidate.

The exact trimer targets retain PBE0, basis/state and stopping-protocol limits;
their pending tighter-stopping comparison is not assumed successful. Canonical
La donor torque and the qualified La electrical discrepancy give independent
support for changing the model. Canonical Dy torque and failed Dy susceptibility
are not silently filled in. None of this uniquely identifies charge transfer,
Pauli effects or anisotropy as the microscopic explanation.

Scientific accountability: I treated shrinking fitting residuals and successful
implementation checks as sufficient reasons to continue that branch. The saved
physical contradictions should instead have triggered this model-form decision.
