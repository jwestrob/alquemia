# Rare-earth biosignature gamble (10 November 2026)

Question: do natural rare-earth patterns carry the two marks that separate protein sites from small chelators in
pure solution (no step between Gd and Tb; yttrium below its size twin)?

Verdict under the rule frozen in `PLAN.md`: **dead**. See `REPORT.md`.

- The two natural biological patterns examined (Deepwater Horizon methane plume; gills of seep mussels with
  methane-eating symbionts) act on La–Nd and stop before Sm.
- Iron oxyhydroxide rejects yttrium more strongly than lanmodulins do, and carbonate complexation shifts every
  binder's reading, so iron oxide in carbonate-bearing water falls in the range predicted for proteins.
- By-product: the natural sink prefers the largest ion (plume removal rate relative to La: Pr 0.26, Nd 0.09,
  Sm 0.00).
